PCSIR Laboratory Testing & Purity Reports

What parameters does PCSIR test in Khalis Bazaar honey?

The Pakistan Council of Scientific & Industrial Research tests for: (1) Sucrose percentage (detects added table sugar or glucose syrup, which must be under 5%—ours tests at 0.0%), (2) Moisture percentage (must be below 20% to prevent fermentation—ours is 16.4%), (3) HMF content (detects heat damage—ours is under 10 mg/kg confirming unheated raw status), and (4) Fructose/Glucose ratio.

Can I test the honey at my own preferred laboratory?

Absolutely yes! We encourage our customers to verify their purchase at any recognized ISO-certified food testing laboratory (e.g. PCSIR, SGS, or Punjab Food Authority labs). If any accredited facility finds artificial sugar, glucose syrup, or adulteration, we will refund your full order price plus your laboratory testing fee.

Honey Crystallization in Winter

My honey has turned thick and granular in cold weather. Has it spoiled or does it contain sugar?

No! In fact, crystallization is the scientific hallmark of 100% pure raw unheated honey. Natural honey is a supersaturated solution of natural fructose and glucose. In temperatures below 20°C, natural glucose separates around microscopic grains of wild bee pollen, forming soft crystals. Commercial brands boil their honey at 70°C+ and micro-filter out pollen so it never crystallizes, but that kills all live enzymes. To reliquefy, simply place the glass jar in a warm water bowl (under 40°C).

Storage & Preservation

Should honey or desi ghee be kept in the refrigerator?

Never refrigerate pure raw honey. Cold temperatures accelerate crystallization and make it harder to spoon. Store raw honey in a dry kitchen cupboard at room temperature away from direct sunlight. Desi cow ghee can also be stored at room temperature in its airtight glass container for up to 12 months.
